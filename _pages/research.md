## A Unified Theory of Jetted TDEs

So far 4 TDEs also boasting a relativistic jet have been observed. In addition, a number of TDEs have a strange radio behavior where there is no radio emission for months and then suddenly radio appears.

<a href="https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.3847/2041-8213/ad0037" class="research-figure--left"><img src="/images/jet.png" alt="A misaligned jet and disk precessing about the spin axis of a supermassive black hole"></a>

In this study, we showed that:

- most TDEs are misaligned with respect to the equatorial plane of the SMBH
- such misaligned jet/disk TDE precess together about the spin axis following Lense-Thirring precession
- the criterion for a precessing jet to escape is much more stringent than for an aligned one.

Even though, most TDEs are born misaligned, we found that two families of mechanisms will align them after some time: a short time for a magneto-spin alignment and a long time for an hydrodynamic alignment. Hence we proposed that each jetted TDEs belonged to one of the 3 classes 1) jet escaping before alignment: J1644, 2) jet escaping after a quick magneto-spin alignment: AT2022cmc or 3) jet escaping after a long hydrodynamic alignment: delayed radio flares.
We predicted that there should be more delayed radio flares than relativistic jets. At the time of our study there were only a few delayed radio flares but in the last 3 years a lot more have been observed!
{: .research-clear}

## Loss Cone Shielding

Centers of galaxies are the densest areas of the universe. Apart from the SMBH, there are a million stars, and thousands of stellar mass black holes all within only 1 pc. All these stars and stellar mass black holes interact through mostly weak (large distance) encounters. Each weak encounter slowly modifies the orbit of stars such that some will after a (number) of interactions have an orbit eccentric enough to become a TDE. Rates of TDEs calculated taking into account those weak encounters are usually one order of magnitude higher than observed rates.

<a href="https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.527.3094T/abstract" class="research-figure--full"><img src="/images/losscone.png" alt="A star on a highly eccentric orbit ejected by a strong encounter instead of being tidally disrupted"></a>

In this study, we proposed that strong (close) encounters also needed to be taken into account to calculate TDE rates. We defined a strong encounter as one encounter close enough to eject the star. We found, that those much rarer strong encounters are very efficient at removing highly eccentric stars that could have otherwise become TDEs. We also showed that by taking into account both weak and strong encounters our theoretical rates are in agreement with observations.

Here is the [link](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.527.3094T/abstract) to the article.
